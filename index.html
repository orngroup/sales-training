The Foundation

Why Enquiries Don't Convert

Most agents answer questions. Great agents sell outcomes.
1
Qualify first, quote second. Understand their dream, budget signal, and travel style before sending any price.
2
Sell the experience, not the itinerary. "Watching the sunrise over Wat Phra Kaew" beats "Day 2: Bangkok sightseeing."
3
Create urgency without pressure. Availability, seasonal pricing, and peak demand are real — use them honestly.
4
Always have a next step. Every conversation must end with a clear action: a call, a decision, a deposit.
5
Follow up is where money lives. 70% of bookings happen on follow-up #2 or later. Don't stop at one call.

The Conversion Funnel

📥 Enquiry received 100%
🗣️ Qualified conversation ~80%
📋 Quote sent ~65%
🔄 Follow-up done ~50%
✅ Deposit received Target: 30%+

Most agents lose at the follow-up stage. This is your biggest opportunity.

Objection Handling — Part 1

Turning "No" Into "Yes"

💰
"Your price is too high / I got it cheaper elsewhere."
Don't defend — differentiate. "I understand price matters. Can I ask what was included in their quote? Our price covers [X, Y, Z]. If we removed those and matched their price, would that be the trip you actually want?" Then reframe around value, not cost.
📅
"Those dates don't work for us."
Offer alternatives immediately — don't ask, suggest. "Absolutely — let me check. We have [alternative dates] which would also catch the Songkran festival, or [other dates] that are slightly off-peak and could save you ₹8,000 per head. Which would suit better?"
🤔
"Let me think about it / I'll get back to you."
Smoke out the real objection. "Of course, no rush. Just so I can help — is it mainly the price, the dates, or something about the itinerary that you'd like to reconsider?" Then solve the actual concern. Always set the follow-up time before hanging up.
👨‍👩‍👧
"I need to discuss with my wife/family."
Invite them in. "That makes complete sense — this is a family holiday. Would it help if I put together a quick visual summary or short video of the itinerary that you could share with her? Sometimes that makes the decision so much easier."
🏨
"I've seen that hotel with poor reviews."
Acknowledge, then offer control. "Good catch — I always appreciate when customers do their research. That particular feedback is often about [specific issue]. I can swap to [alternate hotel] at the same price point, or if you want to stay with the original, I can flag your preferences to the property directly. What would you prefer?"
✈️
"I found flights much cheaper if I book myself."
Let them — then show what they're losing. "Absolutely, you can book flights separately. Our package price includes flights because we hold negotiated fares and it protects you — if a flight is cancelled, we re-book everything together. When you book separately, any disruption and you're handling hotels and transfers on your own. But if you prefer, I can quote land-only too."
Objection Handling — Part 2

More Objections, More Answers

🌧️
"Won't it be rainy / monsoon season in Thailand?"
Turn weather knowledge into confidence. "Great question — Thailand's weather varies by region, which is actually good news. When it's wet in Phuket, Koh Samui is dry, and Bangkok is fine year-round for sightseeing. I'll plan the itinerary around the best weather windows for your exact dates." [Note to team: refer to our destination wiki for precise month-by-month details.]
🍜
"My kids are fussy eaters — will they get Indian food?"
Reassure with specifics. "Thailand is one of the most food-friendly destinations for Indian families. There are Indian restaurants in every major tourist area, and Thai food itself — rice, mild noodles, grilled dishes — kids generally love. I'll also flag any dietary notes to your hotels in advance so they're prepared."
🛡️
"Is Thailand safe? We hear things in the news."
Separate perception from reality. "Thailand is one of the world's top family destinations — millions of Indian tourists visit each year without incident. The tourist areas in Bangkok, Phuket, and Chiang Mai are very well set up. I'll share our destination guide and also include 24/7 emergency support in your package so you always have someone to call."
🛂
"What about visa — is it complicated?"
Make it effortless. "Indian passport holders currently get visa-on-arrival for Thailand — we handle all the documentation guidance, the arrival card, and the fees as part of your package. You just need to carry passport photos and the right amount of cash at the airport. We'll send you a checklist 2 weeks before travel."
📱
"I can plan this myself using Google / Instagram."
Agree — then show what they can't Google. "You absolutely can, and many people do. What you can't Google is our supplier relationships, pre-negotiated rates, and what happens when something goes wrong at 11pm at a Bangkok hotel. We add value precisely at the moments that matter most — and those are the moments you can't plan for."
🔄
"What if we need to cancel or change dates?"
Lead with the policy, close with reassurance. "Our standard terms allow changes up to [X] days before travel. We also offer a travel protection add-on that covers cancellation for medical or family reasons. I'll walk you through both options — most families add it and it's peace of mind for a very small cost."
Key Tactics

Price, Availability & Urgency

When Price is the Issue

💡
Break it down per day. "₹1,20,000 for 2 people sounds large, but it's ₹8,500 per person per day — for luxury accommodation, all transfers, and a private guide. That's less than dinner at a good restaurant."
🔀
Offer a trim, not a slash. "I can bring the price down by ₹12,000 by moving to a 4-star hotel on two nights, or we can keep everything and I'll include a sunset cruise at no extra charge. Which feels better?"
📊
Anchor with a higher option. Always present 3 tiers. The mid-tier converts at the highest rate. The premium tier makes mid look reasonable.

When Dates Are the Issue

📅
Always have two alternatives ready. Before the call, check ±2 weeks on either side. Present both with a brief "reason to consider" for each.
🌊
Link dates to experience. "October in Thailand means fewer crowds, lower prices, and the sea in Krabi is at its clearest. This is actually the better time to go." Make them feel they discovered something.
⏰
Waitlist tactic for sold-out dates. "Those dates are full, but I can put you on priority waitlist — we typically see 1-2 cancellations per departure. Can I hold your name while you confirm the budget?"
⚡
The Golden Rule of Urgency
Never manufacture urgency. But real urgency exists every day — school holiday periods, visa processing times, airline seats, hotel allocations. Know your product and tell the truth. Customers trust you more and act faster.
Know Your Customer

Selling B2B vs B2C — Different Games

🤝

B2B — Sanjay Travels (Trade Agent)

Their priority: Margin, reliability, and ease of booking. They don't care about the romance of Thailand — they care about what they earn and whether you make them look good to their client.
What they want from you: Fast response. Accurate quotes. Flexibility on commission. No surprises at the supplier end.
Your language: "Your margin on this is ₹X per person." "I can hold the price for 48 hours while you close the client." "Let me know if you need a co-branded itinerary."
Never do: Contact their client directly. Undercut them on a direct enquiry from the same customer. Delay a response beyond 4 hours.
👨‍👩‍👧

B2C — Mr & Mrs Garg (Direct Customer)

Their priority: Safety, value for money, and the feeling of being looked after. They don't buy itineraries — they buy the confidence that everything will go smoothly.
What they want from you: To be heard. To feel special. To know you've done this a hundred times and they're in good hands.
Your language: "This is what I'd book for my own family." "I've been to Phuket myself — let me tell you which area of the island to stay in." "I'll be your single point of contact, even after you land."
Never do: Send a generic PDF itinerary without a personal note. Disappear after taking the deposit. Use jargon they don't understand.
🎯 Key insight: B2B is about systems and trust. B2C is about emotion and reassurance. The best agents code-switch effortlessly between the two within minutes of picking up the phone.
Role Play Scenario 1 — B2B

Sanjay Travels · Thailand Enquiry

CUSTOMER TYPE
Trade Agent (B2B)
DESTINATION
Thailand (BKK + Phuket)
PAX
2 Adults + 1 Child (8 yrs)
OBJECTION
Price + Margin concern
🤝
Sanjay (Agent)
Sanjay Travels · Trade Partner
SANJAY
"Bhai, I sent you a Thailand enquiry — family of 3, 7 nights. The client has a budget of around ₹1.5 lakh total. Your quote came at ₹1,85,000. That's too much. Competitor gave ₹1,60,000."
AGENT
"Sanjay bhai, I hear you. Quick question — what's included in the competitor quote? Same hotels, same transfers? Because our quote includes private airport transfers, 4-star hotels in prime locations, and your margin is ₹12,000. If I trim to similar hotels as them, I can bring it to ₹1,62,000 with your ₹10,000 still intact."
SANJAY
"The hotels might be different. But client is asking why so much?"
AGENT
"Perfect — I'll send you two versions: Version A at ₹1,85,000 with the full experience, Version B at ₹1,62,000 with tweaked hotels. Send both to your client. Let them choose — and whichever they pick, your margin stays ₹10K+. I'll have both ready in 30 mins. OK?"
SANJAY
"Ha, that works. Send me both."

What the Agent Did Right

✓
Asked what competitor included — didn't immediately concede on price
✓
Protected Sanjay's margin — made him feel looked after
✓
Offered choice, not a price cut — gave Sanjay a tool to close his client
✓
Set a deadline (30 mins) — maintained momentum
🎭 Practice point: Trainer plays Sanjay. Push back twice more — "Can you do ₹1,55,000?" and "Client wants Phuket only, not Bangkok." Trainee must handle both without dropping margin below ₹8K.

Mr & Mrs Garg · Thailand Family Holiday

CUSTOMER TYPE
Direct (B2C)
PAX
2 Adults, 2 Kids
OBJECTION 1
"Price seems high"
OBJECTION 2
"Need to ask wife"
OBJECTION 3
"Dates not free"
👨‍👩‍👧
Mr Garg
Direct Customer · First-time international family travel
MR GARG
"We want to go Thailand — me, my wife, and two kids age 7 and 10. Looking at May. What will it cost for 8 nights?"
AGENT
"Wonderful choice — Thailand is perfect for families with kids that age! Before I quote, quick question — are you thinking beach-only like Phuket or Koh Samui, or would you like to mix in Bangkok for a day or two? And what kind of experience are you hoping for — relaxed resort stay, or a mix of activities and culture?"
MR GARG
"Mix is fine. But we have a budget of maybe ₹2.5 lakh for everything. And I'll need to confirm with my wife — she'll want to see the hotels."
AGENT
"₹2.5 lakh for 4 pax, 8 nights is very doable — I can build you something really lovely within that. I'll put together a short visual summary with hotel photos that you can share with Mrs Garg tonight. One thing — May is peak school holiday season and hotels book fast. If she likes it, we can hold it with just ₹10,000 for 72 hours. Shall I send it across in the next 2 hours?"
MR GARG
"Yes please. But actually May 20-28 might not work — daughter's exams."
AGENT
"No problem at all — actually, May 28 onwards is even better for Thailand. The first week of June is post-Songkran, prices come down slightly and it's less crowded. I'll show you options for June 1–9 as well in the same brochure. Best of both worlds!"

Debrief — Every Objection Handled

💰
"Budget of ₹2.5 lakh"
Validated it as achievable — no alarm, no pushback. Then immediately pivoted to building value within the budget.
👩
"Need to ask wife"
Turned it into a tool — offered a visual summary she could share. Then used it to introduce urgency (72-hr hold) and a next step.
📅
"Dates might not work"
Reframed the alternative dates as the better option — no apology, just a positive pivot. Included both options in the same output.
🎭 Practice point: Trainer plays Mr Garg. Add: "I saw Thailand for ₹80,000 on MakeMyTrip." Trainee must differentiate without criticising MMT — focus on inclusions, service, and personalisation.
The Close

Your 5-Point Daily Conversion Checklist

1

Respond within 2 hours

Speed signals professionalism. The agent who replies first gets the booking 60% of the time.

2

Qualify before you quote

3 questions minimum: What experience? What dates? What budget signal? Never send a cold quote.

3

Present 3 options, always

Good / Better / Best. Customers who see one price argue about it. Customers who see three pick the middle one.

4

Always end with a next step

"Should I hold this for you?" or "Can I call you Thursday 6pm?" Never leave it open-ended.

5

Follow up 3× before letting go

Day 1, Day 3, Day 7. Each touchpoint adds context — a tip, a new offer, a deadline. Don't just say "just checking in."

Phrases That Close

✓
"Would you like me to hold availability while you confirm?"
✓
"This is a very popular departure — the last 4 seats go quickly after school holiday announcements."
✓
"Can I put your name down — it's not a commitment, just holds the price for 48 hours."
✓
"What's the one thing we'd need to change to make this the right trip for you?"
🏆 Remember: Every "no" is just a "not yet." Your job is to find out what "yet" needs to look like — and then build exactly that.
